Photo story / Tokyo

Shade
and signal.

Tokyo turns from loud to quiet within a few streets, and both versions feel like the same city.

LocationTokyo
Frames5 photographs
ThemeNoise / calm

01 / The neon street

In Akihabara the buildings are covered in light before it is even dark. Arcade signs, game characters and screens stack up the facades until the architecture underneath almost disappears.

Wet pavement makes it louder. Every sign is reflected twice, once above and once below, and the crossing becomes a strip of colour that people walk straight through without looking down.

The noise has an order to it. The signs are stacked floor by floor, each level a different business, and the crossings below are marked so clearly that the crowd moves as if it were following a diagram.

It is the Tokyo that appears first in most photographs, and it is real. But it is only one register of a city that seems to change its volume from block to block.

“Every sign is reflected twice.” Tokyo rarely shows only one version of itself.

02 / Three quieter frames

The city
lowers its voice.

A few stops away: two people walking into the shade of a tree-lined street, a building with a sense of humour, a mirror that turns a crowd into a pattern.

02 / Notes on the frames

On the tree-lined street, two people walking away from the camera are enough to make the road feel like a private route. The city is still there, in the buildings beyond the trees, but the shade changes the scale.

In Kappabashi, the street of kitchen suppliers, one building wears two giant coffee cups as balconies. It is a joke, and an advertisement, and simply a way of making one facade easy to remember among thousands.

In Harajuku the crowd appears in a mirrored entrance, broken into dozens of angled reflections. Nobody in the picture is posing. The building does the composing for them.

03 / A road with no cars

On some afternoons the main street through Akihabara is closed to traffic, and the road from the first frame becomes something else. People walk down the middle. A dog leads its owner past the shops. Nobody hurries.

The signs are still there, but the street has stopped being a route and become a room. It is a small rule, a few hours without cars, and it changes how the whole place feels.

Elsewhere in the album, a small shopping street in Hiroo shows the same idea at a smaller size: one road-closed sign, and the asphalt becomes more pavement.

Seen this way, the neon is not the opposite of the quiet streets. It is the same city, with the volume turned up for a while.

04 / A small conclusion

The quiet is
part of the noise.

Tokyo’s bright side is easy to photograph. The quieter frames take more patience: they are found in the shade, on a balcony shaped like a coffee cup, in the hours when a road belongs to walkers.
