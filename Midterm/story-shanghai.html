Photo story / Shanghai

Two banks
of one river.

Across the Huangpu, the skyline lights up like a screen. Behind it, the city still moves at walking pace.

LocationShanghai
Frames5 photographs
ThemeSkyline / lane

01 / The lit bank

At night the towers of Lujiazui are lit all at once: the pink spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower, columns of blue and violet, screens running across whole facades. The river doubles all of it.

It is one of the most photographed views in the world, and it looks designed to be photographed. Seen from across the water, the skyline is less a group of buildings than a single image.

The light is also a kind of clock. When the screens switch on, the evening has started; later, when they go dark, the skyline turns back into buildings and the river turns back into a working river.

But the people looking at it are standing somewhere older: on the other bank, in a city of plane trees, lanes and small shops that the skyline never shows.

“Less a group of buildings than a single image.” A skyline is a city at its most composed.

02 / Behind the skyline

The city
at walking pace.

A road under plane trees, a lane where bicycles wait outside the doors, a shop window full of folded fans.

02 / Notes on the frames

Wukang Road is lined with plane trees whose branches meet over the road. The houses behind them are low and set back, and the light that reaches the pavement comes through leaves first.

In a lilong lane like Zhenxingli, the space between houses works as a shared hallway. Bicycles lean against the walls, a plant sits on a step, and the line between inside and outside is mostly a matter of where the door happens to be.

In Tianzifang, a maze of old lane houses turned into shops and studios, a small window full of painted fans stops people walking past. It is a souvenir shop, but it is also a reminder that this city is still built at the scale of a doorway.

03 / Five minutes across

The ferries that cross the Huangpu are small, red and white, and unglamorous next to the skyline. This one is heading for the Maqiao dock, far upriver from the towers, where the banks are lined with trees instead of glass. Ferries like it carry commuters, and often their bicycles, from one bank to the other in a few minutes.

Downtown, the same kind of crossing is one of the cheapest views in the city. The famous skyline, seen from the middle of the river, costs the price of a ferry ticket.

From the deck there, both banks are visible at once: the towers on one side, the older waterfront on the other. For a few minutes the city is not split into new and old. It is just two shores and a short trip between them.

04 / A small conclusion

Both banks
are the city.

Shanghai’s bright side is spectacular, and it is only half of the picture. The other half is quieter and easier to miss: the shade on Wukang Road, the lane outside a doorway, the ferry that joins the two.
