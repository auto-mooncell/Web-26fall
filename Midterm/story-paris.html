Photo story / Paris

The street
takes its time.

Paris is famous for a tower, but most of the city is made of long streets and the rooms above them.

LocationParis
Frames5 photographs
ThemeFacade / pause

01 / The long street

Rue Réaumur runs almost straight, and in this frame it is nearly empty: a crosswalk, a few parked scooters, stone facades repeating their balconies all the way to the vanishing point.

Many Paris streets are like this. The buildings agree with each other. They share a height, a colour and a rhythm of windows, so the street reads as one long sentence rather than a list of separate houses.

That agreement makes small differences easy to see: a shop sign, an awning, a single open window on the fourth floor.

Without traffic, the architecture does all the talking: cornices, iron railings, the same pale stone from one end of the street to the other. The emptiness makes the street look older than it is, and calmer than it usually is.

“One long sentence.” A Paris street is easier to read than to describe.

02 / Rooms above the street

Where the city
actually lives.

A corner building with a cafe at its feet, a theatre doorway covered in posters, trees standing in front of a facade like a second wall.

02 / Notes on the frames

On the corner building, the cafe takes the ground floor and the apartments stack above it, their balconies trimmed with iron. The awnings are the brightest colour in the frame, and they mark the place where the street turns from passing through to stopping.

The Café de la Gare doorway is plainer: pale stone, a painted name, a few posters. It is a theatre, and the posters change, but the facade has the patience of a building that expects people to keep coming back.

Near Trocadéro the trees almost hide a corner apartment block. In summer the city adds a second facade made of leaves, and the stone one behind it becomes a background.

03 / Lamps on the bridge

At dusk the lamps of the Pont Alexandre III come on, and the bridge becomes the most decorated object in the frame: gilded figures, iron candelabra, reflections dragged across the Seine.

The Eiffel Tower is in the album too. It is hard to photograph Paris for long without it appearing at the end of a street or over a roof, but it works best as a backdrop.

This is the postcard Paris, and it earns its place. But it works because of everything around it. The ornament stands out only because the streets behind it are so calm.

04 / A small conclusion

The pause
is the point.

Paris rewards slowing down. The tower is visible from far away; the details are only visible from a cafe chair, a doorway, or the middle of an empty street on a quiet afternoon.
