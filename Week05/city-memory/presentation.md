# City, Remembered: Presentation

Target: about five minutes, including pauses to show the sitemap and wireframe.
Read naturally and rehearse once. This text is a proposal, not a claim that the
city website is already built. Review the skills paragraph against what you can
personally explain before presenting.

## 1. Concept and audience (45 seconds)

My midterm project is called City, Remembered. It is a small photography journal
about the details that give a city its character. I want to focus on ordinary
streets, windows, signs, light, and people, rather than only famous landmarks.
The first version will include New York, Tokyo, and Paris. Each city will have
a small photo collection with short captions. My audience is people interested
in photography, design, and travel. I want visitors to slow down and notice
details that they might normally walk past.

## 2. Inspiration (60 seconds)

I have three main references. Thisispaper's travel guides organize places by
city, using large images and short descriptions. I want to borrow that clear
structure for my city album.

Getty's 12 Sunsets lets visitors explore photographs of the same Los Angeles
street across different years. It inspired me to connect photographs with
location and time, and to think about how images preserve a city's memory.
I will borrow that idea, not build its interactive map.

My NYC in Color presents everyday New York life in themed photo collections,
such as subway scenes and street life. I want to use a similar approach to
connect photographs with short stories, rather than only show famous landmarks.
Together, these references inform my city-based structure, photo-led layout,
and focus on everyday urban memories.

## 3. Sitemap and content (45 seconds)

Here is my sitemap. The website will have three pages: Home, City Album, and
Photo Story. Home introduces the project and links to the album and a featured
story. The album contains New York, Tokyo, and Paris as sections on the same
page. These are not three additional pages. City links will jump to those
sections using HTML anchors. I plan to start with three photographs per city.
The story page will focus on New York, reuse selected album photographs, and
include about one hundred and fifty words of original commentary.

## 4. Wireframe and visual direction (50 seconds)

This is my wireframe. The home page starts with simple navigation and a large
street photograph with the project title over it. Below that are three city
previews and a featured story. The album page uses repeated photo and caption
groups, with clear city headings. The story page has a title, a large image,
and a narrow reading column. On a phone, the city previews and photo groups
will become a single column. I want the photographs to stay clear, without
unnecessary effects or complicated animation.

## 5. Current work and learning needs (45 seconds)

My earlier recipe page gives me a starting point. It already contains semantic
HTML, navigation links, an image with alt text, captions, and a linked CSS file.
Its stylesheet also contains spacing, flexbox navigation, and a media query.
For this project, I will reuse those ideas for navigation and photo captions.
I still need more practice with responsive photo layouts, image cropping, and
positioning text over a hero image. I also need to test contrast, keyboard
navigation, and image sizes on different screens. These are the areas where I
would like feedback.

## 6. Scope and next step (35 seconds)

The first version will be built with HTML and CSS. It will not include accounts,
a live map, search, or a JavaScript gallery. I will credit every external photo,
and I will not describe sourced images as photos I took myself. The writing
will be observations inspired by the images, rather than invented travel
memories. After feedback, I will build the page structure, style the shared
navigation and album, and test the site on a laptop and phone. My main question
is whether the three-page scope and the balance between photographs and text
feel realistic for this midterm.
